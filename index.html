The Project Management Lifecycle

A structured framework to guide project teams from initial concept to successful execution and long-term community impact.

1 Initiation
2 Planning
3 Execution
4 M&E (Control)
5 Closure
Phase 1

Project Initiation

Core Question: Is this project worth doing and feasible? Define initial scope, assess community needs, identify key stakeholders, and secure basic authorization.

Needs Assessment: Use surveys, focus groups, and interviews to discover real local problems rather than making assumptions.
Project Charter: Write a high-level project charter defining objectives, expected outcomes, scope boundaries, and core team.
Key Output: An approved Project Charter and stakeholder register to proceed to detailed planning.
Phase 2

Project Planning

Core Question: How exactly will we deliver the project? Map out tasks, milestones, calendars, budgets, resources, and risk mitigations.

Work Breakdown Structure (WBS): Divide the project scope into small, manageable tasks with clear assignments.
Budgeting & Sponsorship: Develop itemized budgets and pitch templates to secure funding and resources.
Key Output: A comprehensive project roadmap (Gantt schedule), budget plans, and risk checklist.
Phase 3

Project Execution

Core Question: Are we deploying planned activities effectively? Coordinate volunteers, manage partners, run public campaigns, and execute operations.

Team Coordination: Hold team standups, distribute volunteer checklists, and manage vendor/partner relations.
Public Relations: Conduct marketing, community outreach, and stakeholder engagement to drive enrollment or attendance.
Key Output: Active delivery of project tasks, workshop sessions, or community campaigns.
Phase 4

Monitoring & Evaluation

Core Question: Are we meeting goals and managing resources? Track timelines, analyze budgets, and gather social impact metrics.

Variance Control: Track actual schedule progress and expenditures vs. target baselines to handle issues early.
Impact Metrics: Verify immediate quantitative outputs and track long-term qualitative changes in communities.
Key Output: Operational data, registry metrics, budget trackers, and feedback surveys.
Phase 5

Project Closure & Handover

Core Question: What did we accomplish and how do we ensure sustainability? Complete accounts, write reports, and hand over assets.

Administrative Closure: Pay final vendor invoices, audit project ledger, and compile financial records.
Lessons Learned & Handover: Archive graphic design assets, contact registers, and write review logs for future teams.
Key Output: Final project evaluation report, lessons log, financial audit, and sustainability handover contracts.

Core Project Management Concepts

Click Triangle Nodes to Explore

Changing any side of the triangle compromises quality.
Scope: The What & How Much Boundary

Scope defines all the work required to complete the project successfully. It is your project's boundary.

Scope Creep: Adding uncontrolled tasks without expanding budget or time. Stop scope creep early to protect your team from burnout.

Time: The Timeline & Schedule Deadline

Projects have a strict start and end. Time management involves planning calendar milestones, tasks duration, and resource scheduling.

Use simple timelines (like Gantt charts) to track milestones and avoid project day crunches.

Cost: The Budget & Funds Finance

Cost encompasses financial budgets, materials, and volunteer assets.

Always draft itemized budgets. Map costs to sponsorships (cash or in-kind donations) and run audits to build credibility with sponsors and partners.

The immediate, tangible results of your activities. Easy to count but doesn't show long-term change.

Outcomes

The actual impact or behavioral shifts in the community. Requires long-term tracking.

Examples: 65% of students secured tech jobs, local coding projects created.

Tying PM Lifecycle & Impact Canvas Into Projects

How does strategy tie into execution? The project lifecycle manages our project phases, the Impact Model Canvas designs our project logic, and strategic governance drives delivery.
